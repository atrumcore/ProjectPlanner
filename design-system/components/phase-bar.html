Anatomy — two rows, always

DEVELOPMENT (Dev env)3 FebT1DEAS
The label owns the top row, across the bar's whole width. The start date and the people chips share a quieter row beneath it — date left, chips right-aligned to the bar's inner edge. Running all three along one line spends the left third of every bar on metadata and truncates the phase name to "SIT (…" while two-thirds of the bar sits empty, so the rows are not optional: they are what keeps the name readable.
Chips are 14px — small enough to read as metadata rather than as the loudest thing on the bar, large enough to still hold two initials at the type scale's 9px floor. Their 1.5px ring is the bar's own surface colour, not white: a white ring makes them look pasted on top of the bar instead of set into it.

Degradation — the name outranks the crowd

As a bar narrows, the meta row gives way before the label does. Chips shed into a +N overflow until the label clears a legible minimum, flooring at one chip so it stays visible that somebody is assigned. Below that minimum the label is dropped rather than drawn as a one-letter stub — the full text is in the tooltip either way. Measured against the real font, never estimated per character (utils/textMeasure), and the label carries no tracking: the type card gives the wide tracking to eyebrows only, and a tracked label overruns the width it was truncated to.
