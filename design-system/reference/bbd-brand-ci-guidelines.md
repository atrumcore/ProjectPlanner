# BBD Brand & CI Guidelines

This Markdown file translates the official BBD Brand Guidelines PDF into a structured, implementation-friendly reference for brand, creative, content, marketing, and documentation teams.

**Important note:**
- Statements presented as **official guidance** come directly from the source PDF.
- Statements presented as **observed visual system** are expert interpretations of repeated design patterns shown in the examples.
- Where the PDF does **not** define a rule explicitly, that gap is called out rather than guessed.

---

## 1. Purpose of the brand guidelines

BBD positions its brand as more than a logo or colour palette. The brand is described as the promise of who the company is, how it shows up, and the value it delivers. The purpose of the guidelines is to protect that promise by ensuring **consistency, clarity, and impact** in every interaction.

## 2. Brand foundation (pp. 3-5)

### 2.1 Who BBD is

BBD is an international software solutions company that partners with clients across industries to design, build, and scale technology that delivers real business value. The company emphasises more than 40 years of experience, combining smart people, proven processes, and modern engineering to solve complex problems and unlock new opportunities.

Its teams operate across cloud-native platforms, digital channels, and enterprise integration, working at the intersection of strategy and execution. BBD explicitly rejects one-size-fits-all solutions in favour of flexible, scalable solutions built to last. The brand also frames itself as a **people business**: passionate technologists working closely with clients to deliver outcomes that matter.

### 2.2 Brand promise

> To simplify complexity and deliver technology solutions that stand the test of time. We bring together the right people, skills, and approach to help clients move faster, stay resilient, and unlock long-term value.

### 2.3 Brand tone

> Our tone is professional, approachable and slightly quirky. We speak with clarity and confidence, but never arrogance. We put people first, using straightforward, human language that connects rather than alienates. Every message should reflect our values: innovative yet grounded, insightful yet practical, and always focused on the value delivered.

### 2.4 Brand values

The guide identifies the following brand values:

- Innovative
- Agile
- Scalable
- Flexible
- Human-centric
- Secure
- Reliable
- Global
- Efficient
- Diverse and inclusive
- Future-proof
- Collaborative
- Sustainable
- Accountability

### 2.5 Strategic interpretation

Taken together, the brand foundation suggests that BBD should consistently present itself as:

- technically credible without sounding cold or abstract
- modern and forward-looking without sounding trend-driven
- human and collaborative rather than corporate and distant
- practical, resilient, and delivery-focused
- international in outlook, but still close to client realities

### 2.6 Messaging implications

Content should consistently reinforce the following themes:

- simplification of complexity
- long-term value, not short-term hype
- partnership and collaboration
- scalable, flexible engineering
- trust, resilience, and reliability
- people-first technology delivery

## 3. Core visual language (observed across the document)

Although the PDF separates strategy, logo, colour, type, imagery, and applications, the examples reveal a consistent visual system.

### 3.1 Observed visual system

- **Dark-first presentation:** Many core pages use near-black backgrounds with white typography and a restrained red accent.
- **Minimal but premium composition:** Large type, generous spacing, and clean layouts create a high-end, confident feel.
- **Red accent as a signal, not a fill colour:** Red is used in short lines, glyph highlights, selected buttons, and small points of emphasis.
- **Rounded corners:** Images, panels, swatches, and gradient containers often use rounded rectangles.
- **Technology with humanity:** Abstract tech imagery and authentic people photography are used together, reinforcing both innovation and human-centred delivery.
- **Pattern language:** Dots, arcs, orbit-like lines, and subtle geometric overlays recur throughout the system.
- **High contrast hierarchy:** Bold separation between black, white, grey, red, and cool blue/teal tones keeps layouts crisp and legible.

### 3.2 Brand personality expressed visually

The visual identity feels:

- modern
- engineered
- polished
- calm
- international
- intelligent
- people-led
- slightly distinctive rather than flashy

## 4. Logo system (pp. 6-10)

### 4.1 Logo history

The guide explains that, in 1984, before BBD was formally established, founders **Sandra, Ralf, and Tom** worked with a designer to create a logo that would signal ambition and substance. The original concept was built around the letters **b, b, and d** orbiting a central sphere to symbolise global reach. Earlier versions included globe lines and geometric letterforms. Over time, this evolved into the current sculptural mark, informally nicknamed **"The Gossamer Orb"**.

### 4.2 Approved logo variants

| Variant | Description | Primary use case |
|---|---|---|
| Primary logo | Full logo with descriptor | Default choice in all instances where the full mark remains legible |
| Secondary logo | Logotype and glyph without the full descriptor treatment | Use when the asset size will not allow the words "software development" to remain readable |
| Glyph only | Standalone symbol | Use only when the asset is too small for the full logo, or when used as a branded design element on items such as stickers or socks |

### 4.3 Default logo decision rule

1. Use the **full logo** by default.
2. If space is too limited for the descriptor to remain readable, use the **secondary version**.
3. If the asset is very small or decorative, use the **glyph only**.

### 4.4 Logo safe space

The source guide specifies a protected area around the logo using a reference unit labelled `X`, with surrounding clear space shown as `5X` in the diagram.

**Working rule for implementation:**
- Maintain a clear exclusion zone of **5X** around the logo on all sides.
- Do not place text, graphics, or page edges inside this protected area.
- The exact construction of `X` is shown visually in the source diagram rather than defined in prose, so production teams should use the official artwork or source files when applying it precisely.

### 4.5 Logo colour applications

Depending on the asset and background, the guide approves these colour treatments to preserve legibility:

- all black
- all white
- white logo with a red glyph

**Selection rule:** choose the approved version that provides the strongest legibility against the background.

### 4.6 Relationship and subsidiary lock-ups

The guide includes three additional lock-ups for contextual use:

| Lock-up | When to use | Typical context |
|---|---|---|
| Group of Companies | To show membership of the wider group | BBD Holdings or partner-company representation |
| Hosted by BBD | When BBD is the host | Meetups or other hosted industry events |
| Powered by BBD | When BBD is the main sponsor | Sponsored events, conferences, Meetups, Escape |

### 4.7 Logo governance notes

The source PDF does **not** explicitly define the following:

- minimum reproduction size
- incorrect logo usage examples
- distortion/stretch rules
- background exclusion rules beyond legibility
- co-branding hierarchy rules
- placement grid for digital layouts

These should be added in a future revision if the brand system is expected to scale across many agencies, partners, or vendors.

### 5.4 Multi-point gradients

The guide visually shows two approved multi-point gradient directions.

#### Multi-point gradient style A

A dark, low-contrast blend built on near-black and charcoal, with subtle cool undertones and a lightly faceted or polygonal texture. This works well for premium, subdued, technology-led backgrounds.

#### Multi-point gradient style B

A brighter cool-tech blend moving through pale silver/white, cyan, teal, blue, and violet. This works well where the brand needs more motion, energy, or digital depth.

**Important:** the extracted text does not provide exact stop values for these multi-point gradients, so production teams should source them from the original artwork files rather than rebuilding them from approximation alone.

### 5.5 Practical colour usage guidance

Based on the PDF examples, the colour system appears to work best when used as follows:

- Use **near-black** as the dominant background for premium, high-contrast layouts.
- Use **white or very light grey** when showcasing imagery, palette, or product/application examples.
- Use **red** sparingly for emphasis: breadcrumb lines, glyph highlights, CTA accents, small calls to action, or visual punctuation.
- Use **deep indigo, blue, teal, and cyan** to create digital energy without overtaking the red accent.
- Avoid turning the system into a rainbow palette; the brand feels strongest when red remains the main signal colour and cool tones remain supportive.

## 6. Typography system (pp. 15-20)

### 6.1 Typeface hierarchy

| Role | Typeface | Weights shown in source | Intended use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary typeface | Montserrat | Thin, Light, Regular, Semibold, Bold, Extrabold, Black | Main brand typeface; broad use across branded communications |
| Secondary typeface | Open Sans | Light, Regular, Semibold, Bold, Extrabold | Supporting typeface for clarity and readability in documents and digital platforms |
| Tertiary typeface | Nordique Pro | Light, Regular, Semibold, Bold | Use sparingly for emphasis, distinction, campaign headlines, and stylised moments |

### 6.2 Official type descriptions

#### Montserrat

Montserrat is described as the core brand typeface: modern and versatile, balancing professionalism with approachability, and creating clarity and consistency across touchpoints.

#### Open Sans

Open Sans is positioned as the secondary face, chosen for clarity, versatility, and readability across documents and digital platforms.

#### Nordique Pro

Nordique Pro is the tertiary face, intended for selective, sparing use where the brand needs a more stylised edge: campaign headlines, creative treatments, or standout moments.

### 6.3 Typography hierarchy rules shown in the guide

#### Main heading breadcrumb

- Indicates the overall theme of the content.
- Uses a **red line above the text**.
- Appears across platforms to reassure users they are in the right space.

#### Sub-heading breadcrumb

- Breaks content into sections or subsections.
- Uses a **vertical line on the left of the text**.
- The line is **shorter and thinner** than the main heading breadcrumb treatment.
- Intended as a consistent sectional device.

#### Headings

- Clearly label sections with emphasis on key words.
- Use **clean, simple typography**.
- Use **no red line**.
- Light and semibold weights are allowed.
- Used throughout content for clarity and scanability.

#### Section content

- Sits beneath headings or breadcrumbs.
- Provides the main body information.
- Should allow dense sections to breathe.
- Must remain **left-aligned**.

#### CTA section heading

- Draws attention to call-to-action content.
- Uses a **red line above the text**.
- Applied consistently wherever CTA sections appear.

#### CTA button

- Supports direct user interaction such as "sign up" or "learn more".
- Both **dark** and **light** styles are shown as approved.
- Should remain standardised across platforms.

### 6.4 Typography behaviour inferred from layouts

The examples suggest the following practical rules:

- Prefer **clean line breaks** and strong vertical rhythm over tight, compressed paragraphs.
- Use large, airy display type on cover and section-intro pages.
- Keep body copy measured and readable rather than overly dense.
- Use typography to create hierarchy before adding extra graphic treatment.
- Reserve Nordique Pro for rare, deliberate moments; overuse would dilute its impact.

### 6.5 Recommended font fallback logic for digital systems

If a Markdown-rendered environment cannot serve the brand fonts directly, use this fallback order:

1. `Montserrat` for headings and primary UI text
2. `Open Sans` for body copy
3. `Nordique Pro` only where licensed and technically supported
4. system sans-serif fallback if fonts cannot be loaded

## 8. Imagery system (pp. 22-25)

### 8.1 Abstract backgrounds

The abstract imagery examples focus on:

- close-up, high-detail technology references
- luminous motion and light trails
- reflective or glossy surfaces
- biometric or futuristic textures
- a premium, immersive digital feel

**Use case:** these backgrounds are well suited to hero sections, campaign visuals, high-impact social content, event materials, and presentation separators.

### 8.2 People imagery - primary

The primary people imagery shows:

- authentic individuals and teams
- natural or softly lit environments
- collaborative and candid moments
- professional but approachable styling
- visible diversity across age, gender, and ethnicity

**Interpretation:** the primary people imagery should feel real, human, and credible rather than overly staged or stock-like.

### 8.3 People imagery - secondary

The secondary people imagery uses:

- dramatic red backgrounds or red lighting
- more stylised portraiture
- stronger contrast and mood
- a bolder campaign-like feel

**Interpretation:** this imagery tier is suitable when the brand needs more punch, distinction, or thematic cohesion with red-led campaign moments.

### 8.4 Pattern imagery

The pattern examples suggest a reusable library based on:

- dots and circular arrays
- arcs and orbital curves
- thin lines in red, white, and grey
- black and white fields with occasional red highlights
- abstract geometric motion

These patterns are useful as overlays, backgrounds, dividers, or layout enhancers.

### 8.5 Imagery selection principles inferred from the examples

When choosing or commissioning imagery, prioritise the following:

- authenticity over generic corporate stock
- diversity and inclusivity
- collaboration and concentration
- modern work and technology contexts
- premium digital abstraction rather than clichéd tech visuals
- strong contrast and clean cropping
- rounded corners where the broader layout system calls for them

## 11. Brand voice guide for writers and marketers

This section converts the source tone guidance into practical writing behaviour.

### 11.1 Voice characteristics

| Do sound like | Avoid sounding like |
|---|---|
| confident | arrogant |
| human | overly technical for its own sake |
| practical | vague or conceptual-only |
| insightful | preachy |
| modern | trendy or gimmicky |
| approachable | casual to the point of losing authority |
| slightly distinctive | quirky for the sake of being quirky |

### 11.2 Writing principles

- Start with value and clarity.
- Explain complexity simply.
- Put the client or audience need first.
- Use plain English where possible.
- Keep claims grounded in expertise, delivery, and outcomes.
- Balance innovation language with proof, trust, and practicality.

### 11.3 Recommended message structure

A strong BBD message typically follows this arc:

1. the challenge or need
2. the practical solution or expertise
3. the business value or outcome
4. the invitation to act or continue the conversation

## 12. Markdown implementation notes

Because you asked for a Markdown version specifically, this section explains how to preserve the CI system when the brand is implemented in Markdown-based documentation, internal knowledge bases, or Git-driven content systems.

### 12.1 Important limitation of Markdown

Plain Markdown controls **content structure**, not full visual brand expression. Fonts, breadcrumb lines, rounded cards, CTA button styles, and colour tokens must usually be applied through:

- site CSS
- documentation theme settings
- MDX / HTML wrappers
- design-system components
- static-site generator templates

### 12.2 Recommended heading mapping for Markdown docs

| Brand concept | Markdown / docs equivalent | Styling should be handled by |
|---|---|---|
| Main heading breadcrumb | `#` page title plus a themed kicker/eyebrow label | CSS / template |
| Sub-heading breadcrumb | `##` or `###` with section kicker treatment | CSS / component |
| Standard heading | semantic Markdown headings | theme typography |
| Section body | normal paragraph text | theme body styles |
| CTA section heading | callout block or highlighted subsection | theme callout styles |
| CTA button | styled link component or HTML button class | docs theme / CSS |

### 12.3 Suggested design tokens for Markdown-powered sites

```css
:root {
  --bbd-black: #151719;
  --bbd-red: #CE181E;
  --bbd-deep-blue: #1D1950;
  --bbd-light-grey: #DCDDDE;
  --bbd-mid-grey: #8A9298;
  --bbd-dark-grey: #343442;

  --bbd-teal-dark: #006166;
  --bbd-teal-mid: #25898E;
  --bbd-cyan: #00B8CC;
  --bbd-indigo: #37309C;
  --bbd-violet-blue: #4E44DB;

  --bbd-font-heading: "Montserrat", "Open Sans", Arial, sans-serif;
  --bbd-font-body: "Open Sans", Arial, sans-serif;
  --bbd-font-accent: "Nordique Pro", "Montserrat", Arial, sans-serif;
}
```

### 12.4 Suggested Markdown authoring rules

- Use semantic heading levels consistently.
- Keep paragraphs relatively short for scanability.
- Left-align all body copy.
- Avoid deep nesting unless the content genuinely needs it.
- Use images intentionally rather than decoratively.
- Add alt text for accessibility.
- Keep CTA links consistent in wording.
- Do not try to mimic branded buttons in raw Markdown unless your renderer supports it cleanly.

### 12.5 Sample Markdown page pattern

```md
# Cloud-native modernisation

A practical guide to simplifying complex transformation work.

## Why organisations choose BBD

We combine engineering depth, scalable delivery, and long-term thinking to help teams move faster with confidence.

## What we deliver

- Strategy-to-execution partnership
- Flexible engineering teams
- Modern software delivery
- Long-term business value

## Next step

[Talk to BBD](#)
```

### 12.6 If HTML is allowed inside Markdown

If your Markdown renderer supports HTML, you can express more of the brand system through reusable wrappers such as:

```html
<div class="bbd-kicker">TYPOGRAPHY</div>
<h1>Main heading</h1>
<p>Body copy goes here...</p>
<a class="bbd-button" href="#">Visit website</a>
```

This is often the most reliable way to carry the identity into documentation sites without compromising semantic structure.

## 13. Quick-reference checklist

### 13.1 Always do

- use the full logo first, then step down only when space requires it
- keep the logo clear and legible
- use red as a deliberate accent
- pair technology imagery with human credibility
- keep typography clean and spacious
- write with clarity, confidence, and warmth
- keep content left-aligned and easy to scan
- use authentic, diverse imagery

### 13.2 Avoid

- overloading layouts with too many colours
- using red everywhere until it loses emphasis value
- choosing imagery that feels generic or overly staged
- sounding arrogant or buzzword-heavy
- using stylised typography everywhere
- crowding the logo or placing it in low-contrast situations
- turning the brand into a purely abstract tech aesthetic without people
